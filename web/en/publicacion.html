    Hispanidad as a Historiographical Problem

    Authorship editable from the CMS · Received: 2026 · Accepted: 2026

    ABSTRACT

    This demo article reviews the uses of the concept of Hispanidad in the last century's historiography — from interwar essay-writing to connected histories — and proposes a framework that treats it as an object of study rather than a banner. The real text will be loaded from the content manager.

    Keywords: historiography Hispanic world Atlantic history political concepts

    1. Introduction

    Few concepts in Spanish-language culture have accumulated as many layers of meaning as Hispanidad. Coined, debated, celebrated and challenged throughout the 20th century, the term now works less as an answer than as a question: what do the societies that recognise themselves in a shared history and language have — and not have — in common?

    2. State of the question

    From Ramiro de Maeztu's Defensa de la Hispanidad (1934) to Hugh Thomas's syntheses on the Spanish empire, by way of the renewal of Atlantic history and connected histories, the bibliography available in the Chair's collection shows the breadth of the debate.

    3. Proposed framework

    The final section would develop the proposed analytical framework: treating Hispanidad as a historical network of circulations — people, norms, images, words — whose density varies over time and space, and which can be measured, mapped and discussed with the tools of the contemporary humanities.
